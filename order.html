The Order of the Dawn

"The Dawn does not deal in mercy. It deals in light."

For four centuries the Order has kept the Temple of Dawn on the hill above the Lower Town. It feeds the poor, buries the dead, and guards the one thing the Valelands trust more than their kings: the Vault.

The Vault of Dawn

Every tithe, offering and bequest given to the Order rests in the Vault beneath the temple. The coin is counted once a quarter, before witnesses, and the count is read aloud from the temple steps.

The Vault has one door and three locks. All three keys open all three locks; any single key-bearer may enter alone.

The Three Keys

The keys are given at a rite and bound to the soul of the bearer. A key turns for no hand but its bearer's. It cannot be lent, copied or stolen, and it crumbles to ash when its bearer dies.

Key-bearerWhy they hold a key
The High PriestTo give alms and answer for the Order's purse
The QuartermasterTo feed, clothe and repair
The ChampionTo arm the Order in time of war. By custom the Champion's key is never used in peacetime

There has never been a fourth key.

The Guardian

The door is watched by the Guardian, a thing of bronze older than the temple. It marks every opening of the door in the Vault's ledger: the day, and the weight of coin that left. It does not write who entered. The founders forbade it to name a key-bearer, so that none could be accused by a machine.

The Guardian may be asked one kind of question, and it answers truthfully: how many hands.

Seals of Purpose

A key-bearer who takes coin from the Vault writes the purpose beside the Guardian's mark and sets their seal on it. An opening with no seal of purpose is, in the Order's law, a theft.
